🔔 Morning Watchlist — where to look at the open

Three questions only: what happened · how big · can I trade it. Everything else about the news is deliberately not here. It keeps updating all session — LUMAXTECH published Q1 at 09:41 and ran 3.8%, which no pre-open list could have caught. Anything arriving after 09:15 is flagged NEW and sounds an alarm.
Ranks 1·2·3 are conviction in the setup — not odds of profit. Before 09:15 the rank uses the news alone. After the open it re-ranks on the actual tape: a bullish name that is falling gets penalised hard enough to drop out of the top 3, because people with money on it have already disagreed with the headline.
This tells you where to look, never what will hold. On 11-Aug KSHINTL would have ranked near the top and still faded from +5.65% to +0.76% by 09:26, while INDSWFTLAB held +7.5%. Nothing knowable before the open separated them.

🎯 Premarket Predictor — the 09:00 call vs what actually happened

▶how this is scored
Window: news published between the previous session's close (15:30) and today's open (09:15) — the window that explains today's gaps. Impact = predicted direction Chance = % likelihood, text only, final before 09:08 Severity = 0–100, how BIG the news is
Chance is a MEASURED FREQUENCY, not a model — and it is self-calibrating. It reports how often calls of this type have actually been right on the tradeable (open→close) move, from your own accumulated outcomes, with the sample size and 95% interval printed underneath. As sessions accumulate it corrects itself. It uses nothing that arrives after 09:08, so the number at 20:00 the night before is the number at 09:10.

Why it is not a clever model: three attempts to find an edge all failed. text severity → 55% / 54% / 55% across bands — no discrimination fixing the headline classifier → 52 calls changed, 7 fixed / 7 broken fade-the-gap on 96 gaps ≥2% → −0.232%/trade, 43% wins Good news on a small-cap resolves near a coin flip on the leg you can actually trade. Rather than dress a fitted guess up as a probability, this reports the observed rate and its uncertainty.

Read the interval, not the number. "57% · 47–67% · n=89" means the true rate could be anywhere in that band. The old scale printed 92% on the same data whose measured rate is 54%, and its 80–100 bucket was its worst performer. Range is clamped to 35–70 because nothing observed has ever justified more.

Severity leads the sort, not Chance. Chance now takes only a few distinct values, so it tells you how much to trust a call; severity tells you which headline is bigger.

CONFIRMS / CONTRADICTS under Chance is the pre-open auction, reported separately. Folding it in lifted the day-% hit rate 78%→93% but the tradeable rate only 53%→55%: it was predicting the opening gap and taking credit for it.
Two reality numbers, not one — and they disagree a lot. Day % is prev-close→now, which includes the opening gap. But the gap happened before 09:15, so you could not have traded it. Tradeable % is open→now — the only part a 09:00 call could actually have captured. Across the 6 recorded sessions the day-% hit rate is 80% and the tradeable hit rate is 54%. The 26-point difference is gap that was already priced in.

Verdicts: HIT = right way and bigger than the ₹10–11 round-trip cost (0.105%). TOO SMALL = right way, under cost — correct but unprofitable. GAP-FADED = opened the right way then reversed. MISS = wrong way.

Historic sessions were backfilled from the board's own logs, so only stocks that appeared on the board that day carry a reality figure (~20 of 60 per session). From now on the live scorer fills every call every 5 minutes.
